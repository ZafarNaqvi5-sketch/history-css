# India–Pakistan Bilateral Relations (High-Yield)

This is one of the **most important** topics for CSS (Pakistan Affairs + Current Affairs + IR).

---

## Core Issues

1. **Kashmir / Jammu & Kashmir**
2. **Cross-border terrorism / militancy**
3. **Water** (Indus Waters Treaty)
4. **Nuclear stability**
5. **Trade & people-to-people**
6. **Diplomatic engagement vs. confrontation cycles**

---

## Major Phases

### 1947–1971
- Partition violence
- First Kashmir War → Ceasefire Line
- 1965 War → Tashkent
- 1971 War → Bangladesh + Simla Agreement (bilateral framework established)

### 1972–1998
- Simla as the guiding framework
- Periodic crises but no full-scale war
- Nuclear programs developing on both sides

### 1998–1999
- Nuclear tests (May 1998)
- Lahore Declaration (Feb 1999) — high point of diplomacy
- Kargil (May–Jul 1999) — major setback

### 2000s
- 2001 Parliament attack → military standoff
- Composite Dialogue process (on-off)
- 2008 Mumbai attacks → major rupture

### 2010s–Present
- Surgical strikes (2016), Balakot (2019)
- Article 370 abrogation (2019) — strong Indian position
- Trade restrictions, reduced diplomatic engagement
- Periodic LoC ceasefire understandings
- Ongoing tensions over terrorism and Kashmir

## Key Agreements (Quick Recall)

| Agreement              | Year     | Core Point |
|------------------------|----------|------------|
| Indus Waters Treaty    | 1960     | River sharing (World Bank) |
| Tashkent Declaration   | 1966     | Post-1965 withdrawal |
| Simla Agreement        | 1972     | Bilateral resolution; LoC |
| Lahore Declaration     | 1999     | Peace & CBMs after nuclear tests |

---

## Enduring Patterns

- **Cycles** of engagement → crisis → freeze → limited thaw
- Kashmir remains the core dispute
- Terrorism is India’s primary stated concern
- Water treaty has been one of the more resilient frameworks (though recently politicized)
- Nuclear weapons raised the cost of full-scale war
- Domestic politics on both sides heavily influence the relationship
