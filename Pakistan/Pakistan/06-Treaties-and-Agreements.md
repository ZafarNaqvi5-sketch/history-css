# Pakistan — Key Treaties & Agreements

**Must-remember for CSS** (especially Pakistan Affairs & International Relations)

---

## 1. Indus Waters Treaty (1960)

| Item              | Detail |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Signed**        | 19 September 1960 (Karachi) |
| **Parties**       | India & Pakistan (brokered by World Bank) |
| **Signatories**   | Jawaharlal Nehru (India), Ayub Khan (Pakistan) |
| **Key Provisions**| Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) → Pakistan<br>Eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) → India |
| **Mechanism**     | Permanent Indus Commission |
| **Significance**  | One of the most durable water-sharing treaties in the world; survived multiple wars. (Status has faced recent political challenges.) |

---

## 2. Tashkent Declaration (1966)

| Item              | Detail |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Signed**        | 10 January 1966 |
| **Place**         | Tashkent (then USSR, now Uzbekistan) |
| **Parties**       | India (Lal Bahadur Shastri) & Pakistan (Ayub Khan) |
| **Mediator**      | Soviet Premier Alexei Kosygin |
| **Key Points**    | Withdrawal of forces to positions before 5 Aug 1965; restoration of diplomatic relations; non-interference |
| **Note**          | Shastri died in Tashkent the next day. |

---

## 3. Simla Agreement (1972)

| Item              | Detail |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Signed**        | 2 July 1972 |
| **Place**         | Shimla, India |
| **Parties**       | India (Indira Gandhi) & Pakistan (Zulfikar Ali Bhutto) |
| **Key Provisions**| - Disputes to be resolved bilaterally<br>- Ceasefire Line → Line of Control (LoC)<br>- Respect for each other’s territorial integrity<br>- No unilateral action to change the situation |
| **Significance**  | Cornerstone of post-1971 bilateral framework. Both sides have at times questioned its status in political rhetoric. |

---

## 4. Lahore Declaration (1999)

| Item              | Detail |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Signed**        | 21 February 1999 |
| **Parties**       | India (Atal Bihari Vajpayee) & Pakistan (Nawaz Sharif) |
| **Context**       | Post-nuclear tests (1998); Vajpayee’s bus diplomacy |
| **Key Points**    | Commitment to peace, composite dialogue (including Kashmir), nuclear confidence-building measures, non-use of force |
| **Aftermath**     | Kargil conflict erupted months later, undermining the spirit of the declaration. |

## Quick Memory Chain

**War → Agreement**
- 1947–48 → UN Ceasefire Line (1949)
- 1965 → Tashkent (1966)
- 1971 → Simla (1972)
- Nuclear tests 1998 → Lahore Declaration (1999) → Kargil

**Water** → Indus Waters Treaty (1960) — longest surviving major bilateral treaty.
