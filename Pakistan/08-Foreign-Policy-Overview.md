# Pakistan — Foreign Policy Overview (High-Yield)

**Core principle historically**: Security-centric, especially India-focused; search for external balancers.

---

## Major Pillars / Phases

### 1. Early Years (1947–1958)
- Sought Western alliances for security against India.
- Joined **SEATO** (1954) and **Baghdad Pact / CENTO** (1955).
- Close ties with USA.

### 2. Ayub Era
- Still pro-West but began opening to China (especially after 1962 Sino-Indian war).
- 1963 Boundary Agreement with China.

### 3. 1971 & Bhutto
- Loss of East Pakistan → reorientation.
- Closer to China and Muslim world.
- Started nuclear program in earnest.

### 4. Zia Era (1979–1988)
- Soviet invasion of Afghanistan → Pakistan becomes frontline state.
- Massive US aid + Saudi support.
- Support to Afghan Mujahideen.
- Nuclear program advances quietly.

### 5. Post-Cold War & 1990s
- US sanctions after nuclear tests (Pressler, etc.).
- Looking for diversification.

### 6. Musharraf & Post-9/11
- Major shift: alliance with USA in War on Terror.
- Economic and military benefits + domestic costs (terrorism blowback).

### 7. 21st Century
- **China** becomes the most reliable strategic partner (CPEC / BRI).
- Balancing USA, China, Saudi Arabia, and managing India & Afghanistan.
- Economic diplomacy increasingly important (IMF, Gulf, China).

## Key Organizations

- **OIC** — Important platform
- **SCO** — Member
- **SAARC** — Largely stalled due to India–Pakistan tensions
- **ECO** — Economic Cooperation Organization
- **CPEC / BRI** — Flagship economic-strategic project with China
- **UN** — Consistent engagement, especially on Kashmir

---

## Enduring Themes

1. **India factor** dominates strategic thinking.
2. Search for external balancer (USA → China).
3. Afghan policy has repeatedly backfired (blowback).
4. Economic weakness limits foreign policy autonomy.
5. Nuclear capability changed the cost of full-scale war.
